Constellation EnergyNASDAQ: CEG

Constellation Energy: Upside Without Beating the Street · by Ethan Jin

Constellation Energy is the largest nuclear operator in the U.S. Its contracted base earnings are set to grow about 23% a year, from roughly $7.65 per share in 2027 to $11.40 - $11.90 in 2029, as new Power Purchase Agreements (PPAs), uprates, and credit-backed revenue come online. However, the market has already noticed. We rate CEG an Accumulate. Our 2027 and 2028 EPS estimates sit about 8% and 14% below consensus, but our upside doesn’t rely on earnings beating the Street. Instead, the stock currently trades at 18.8x our 2028E EPS, below its 23x forward P/E median since 2022. Combined with the recent pullback, a return to 22.5x implies about 20% upside.

Investment summary

  • Long-term contracts are turning Constellation’s nuclear output into stable, long-dated revenue. CEG has recently signed long-term agreements with Amazon (190 MW, Sept 2026), Google (890 MW, Oct 2026), Meta (1,121 MW, Jun 2025), and Microsoft (835 MW, Sept 2024): some of the market’s most ambitious hyperscalers.
  • Zero Emission Credit (ZEC) policies are providing Constellation with a stable earnings floor. New York recently renewed their Zero Emission Credit Program to 2049. However, such policies don’t have such longevity in other key states. Illinois, where CEG hosts six nuclear plants, currently features two programs: ZECs and Carbon Mitigation Credits (CMCs) for five of CEG’s Illinois plants. Both programs expire in 2027, and renewal is uncertain.
  • Contracted base earnings are set to compound at around 23% per year. Base EPS is expected to rise from roughly $7.65 in 2027 to $11.40 - $11.90 in 2029.
  • We rate CEG an Accumulate, with a 12-month target of $329.63. With a five-year median forward P/E ratio of about 23x, we apply a 22.5x multiple to our 2028E EPS to get our target share price. This is a ~47% premium to VST’s 15.3x multiple, which we attribute to CEG’s larger nuclear fleet and breadth of hyperscaler relationships.

Key risks

  • Power prices are volatile. CEG’s Q2 earnings presentation states that sensitivity is about $0.45 of EPS per $1 / MWh. At 22.5x, each $1 / MWh miss is worth about $10 per share.
  • Policy support is uncertain. While New York ZECs have been extended until 2049, Illinois support ends in 2027 and the §45U credit runs only through 2032 and shrinks when power prices are high.
  • Leverage is high. After the Calpine acquisition, net debt rose by 348.57% to $24.0B, so the stock is highly exposed to interest rates and any miss in cash flow.
  • Execution on growth. Delays in restarts and uprates happen, and projects can take years to get approved. A delay moves earnings out in time, which could affect stock prices in key years like 2028 and 2029.
