First SolarNASDAQ: FSLR

Policy Tailwinds Behind America's Largest Solar Maker · by Ethan Jin

FSLR is America’s leading photovoltaic (PV) solar technology manufacturing company, and the only U.S. headquartered company among the world’s largest solar manufacturers. Unlike its foreign competitors, FSLR’s modules contain 0% polysilicon and primarily consist of cadmium telluride (CdTe), reducing exposure to harsh tariffs and Foreign Entity of Concern (FEOC) classifications. We rate FSLR a Buy with a 12-month base-case target of $234.72, representing 36.9% upside on its November 13, 2026 closing price.

Investment summary

  • FSLR is the one large-scale U.S. module maker which has minimal reliance on Chinese trade. Because their modules don’t contain any polysilicon, FSLR doesn’t engage in trade with Xinjiang, which manufactures 35 – 45% of the world’s polysilicon. This allows FSLR’s costs to be minimally impacted by Section 232, Section 301, and FEOC rules while similar-scale competitors are heavily affected.
  • Incentive programs provide multi-year earnings tailwinds through 2029. In FSLR’s two most relevant markets, the United States and India, government production credits provide FSLR with per watt credits for simply manufacturing solar modules in domestic American and Indian facilities.
  • Prices of solar modules are expected to rise. From December 4, 2026, onwards, solar module imports must be priced at a minimum of $0.38/W and $0.22/W for cells plus a 15% tariff. For U.S. module manufacturers who import solar cells, Roth Capital Partners estimates that post-232 pricing may rise to $0.40/W. FSLR makes its own thin-film modules in the U.S., so their asking price can rise while costs stay flat.
  • We rate FSLR a Buy with a 12-month target of $234.72 and 36.9% implied upside. We used a forward EV/EBITDA multiple for our price target, with DCF valuation and comparable analysis as cross checks.

Key risks

  • Post-credit pricing and margins (High) The 45X credit steps down to 75% in 2030, 50% in 2031, and 25% in 2032, before vanishing altogether in 2033. The backlog is priced at $0.31/W, and against a production cost of $0.267/W, that leaves an ex-credit margin of only $0.04/W.
  • Demand after developer credits end (Medium-High) New solar projects may find it hard to qualify for investment and production tax credits. Backlog fell by 1.1 GW last quarter while 4.1 GW were shipped, which implies about 3 GW of net new orders.
  • Multiple compression (Medium) Every 1.0x above or below is worth about $34 a share. If it falls to our 5.0x bear case, base-case EBITDA still gives about $203, 18% above the current price, so the upside would shrink but not disappear.
  • Policy and trade (Low) The 45X credit was about 82% of gross profit in the first nine months of 2026, so any change to it, to the FEOC rules that govern which customers claim credits, or to the Section 232 import floor would hit FSLR’s earnings directly.
